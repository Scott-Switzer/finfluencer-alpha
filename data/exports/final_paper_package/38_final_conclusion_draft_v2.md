# Final Conclusion (v2)

The audit of the FIN 496 empirical package finds that the committed locked artifact package associates transcript-supported YouTube financial recommendations with significant short-window abnormal returns. The headline association (0.52%) is robust to timing and SEC-only filters, but the current free-news layer is only a simulated diagnostic scaffold and the 8,994 transcript count remains a sample-lock limitation. The result remains fundamentally tied to mega-cap technology momentum.

The failure of the association in non-top tickers and its extreme sensitivity to transaction costs preclude any claim of broad "tradable alpha." Instead, the results support a model of **attention amplification**, where social media content synchronizes and intensifies retail interest in high-salience assets. This study provides a manifest-supported event-panel framework for auditing digital influence and underscores the need for high-fidelity news and pricing data (e.g., Bloomberg) to further isolate social media effects from the broader information environment.
