# Final Abstract

This study investigates the association between YouTube-based financial recommendations and short-term abnormal stock returns using a locked sample of 8,994 English-language transcripts and 1,554 distinct recommendation events. We identify a statistically significant positive association in the canonical baseline, with a mean 5-day abnormal return of 0.52% (p=0.001). This association remains robust to timing-lookahead filters and SEC-only robustness tests (where abnormal returns increase to 0.80% for events without nearby official filings). 

However, deeper heterogeneity analysis reveals that the positive signal is heavily dependent on a "Top 5" group of high-momentum technology stocks (NVDA, TSLA, AAPL, AMD, AMZN). When these tickers are excluded, the association for the remaining sample becomes significantly negative (-0.68%, p=0.002). Furthermore, calendar-time portfolio diagnostics demonstrate extreme sensitivity to transaction costs, with alpha effectively vanishing at 25 basis points. We conclude that YouTube-based financial content functions as an **attention amplifier** around high-salience momentum stocks rather than a provider of broad, tradable, or causal alpha.
