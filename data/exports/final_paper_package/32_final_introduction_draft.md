# Final Introduction

The rise of social media as a primary source of financial information for retail investors has raised critical questions about market efficiency and the quality of digital financial advice. While previous research has focused on short-form platforms like X (formerly Twitter) and Reddit, the impact of long-form video content on YouTube—which often provides more detailed rationale and evidence—remains less understood.

This study asks whether transcript-supported YouTube financial recommendations are associated with short-window abnormal stock returns. Using a reproducible NLP pipeline and an event-study framework, we analyze a locked sample of over 1,500 recommendations across 35 prominent creators. 

We frame our findings within the "attention-induced price pressure" hypothesis. Our results suggest that while YouTube creators are synchronized with meaningful price movements, the signal is best interpreted as the amplification of existing sector momentum in mega-cap tech stocks. By subjecting the data to rigorous timing, news, and concentration stress tests, we provide a conservative and nuanced view of the role of social media in modern price formation.
