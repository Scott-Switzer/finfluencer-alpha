# Final Discussion Section

Our findings suggest that YouTube financial content acts as a powerful **attention amplifier** rather than a source of unique fundamental insight. The concentration of the positive association in high-profile technology stocks aligns with behavioral theories where retail attention is drawn to stocks with high salience and existing momentum.

The reversal of the signal for non-top tickers is particularly noteworthy. It suggests that for smaller stocks, social media recommendations may induce temporary price pressure that quickly mean-reverts, a finding consistent with the "pump and dump" or "attention-driven overvaluation" literature.

Furthermore, the extreme sensitivity to transaction costs highlights the gap between statistical association and economic tradability. The high-turnover nature of following YouTube signals, combined with the decay of alpha at 25 bps, makes it unlikely that the identified association represents a viable path to idiosyncratic excess returns for the average investor.
