# Factor-Adjusted Alpha Interpretation

## Survival Analysis (at 5% significance)

### What Survives?
- **1-Day Horizon (Canonical)**: FF3 (p=0.03), Carhart (p=0.04), and FF5 (p=0.002) models show significant positive alpha.
- **5-Day Horizon (Low-Lookahead)**: The Carhart 4-factor model (FF3 + Momentum) remains significant (p=0.038), suggesting the signal is not just a simple momentum play in this cleaner sample.
- **1-Day Horizon (Low-Lookahead)**: FF3 (p=0.038) and FF5 (p=0.032) survive.

### What Fails?
- **Duplicate-Collapsed Sample**: All factor models fail to find significant alpha at both 1D and 5D horizons (all p-values > 0.39). This suggests that the aggregate alpha in the headline sample is heavily dependent on the "echo chamber" effect of multiple creators discussing the same stock.
- **Non-Top Ticker Sample**: Alpha not only fails but becomes **significantly negative** (p=0.002 to p=0.045). This implies that for smaller, less-liquid, or less-covered stocks, YouTube recommendations are associated with immediate and persistent underperformance.

## Theoretical Implications
1. **Alpha vs. Attention**: The signal appears to be more "attention" than "alpha." The fact that it survives FF5 (which includes quality and investment factors) but fails duplicate-collapse suggests the return is a function of the *volume* of social media activity rather than unique fundamental insight.
2. **Momentum and Reversal**: The Carhart results suggest the 5D return in the low-lookahead sample is distinct from broad market momentum. However, the non-top-ticker reversal suggests a "pump and fade" dynamic for stocks outside the mega-cap tech circle.
3. **Mega-Cap Dependency**: The entire positive alpha result is contingent on a handful of high-beta tech stocks. Investors "following" these recommendations are essentially doubling down on existing sector momentum rather than identifying mispriced idiosyncratic opportunities.
